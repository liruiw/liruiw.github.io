RFM Commercialization

January 2026

While all companies aspired to building the most general robot brains for all tasks and environments in robotics and building the most capable humanoid hardware that is super-human, the challenges in generalization and performance for real-world physics is real. So the time and breakthroughs that we will reach those general applications as of today can be quite challenging. There are two ways or steprocks that people often put in commercializing the current robot capabilities to reach the fully general robot hardware/software (home butlers as in all roboticists' textbooks). These are also new applications that highlight the breakthrough in RFMs and general foundation models in the past few years.

The first one is to-C consumer hardware: Leveraging the multimodal capabilities of RFMs to build a companion or small home workers that people will buy at home. These robots do not need to do fully autonomous house works at the beginning, but they need to be interactive with people. It needs to make people feel comfortable of having them at home and likely provide some emotional values. They often can talk and listen (with audio modes) as well as see (video streaming), and then act (with certain pre-programmed skills or real-time behaviors). Not to mention these hardware can provide other applications such as entertainment, education, surveillence. It would not be crazy for families in the silicon valley for pay a few hundred bucks for this cute hardware at home. Some challenges will include the user-retentions of consumer hardware and the motion side of these hardware seem less appealing for home applications today.

The second one is to-B labor business: Leveraging the dexterous capabilities of RFMs to address a single industry. One of the tasks that all companies do today is cloth folding, which is arguably a real-world needs for public laundry places in the north america. These kinds of dexterous capabilities are new: and they directly can be used at the business without millions of dollars for specialized machines, which make them more attractive to small-to-medium business. The business expansion side of these robots can allow for Raas or full software licensing kinds of ways for earning money. There are many more applications like these in the world, and the recipe of getting a model to do such tasks reliably (and even bootstraped from human teleoprations) are within reach. Some problems to address are the costs of expansion and efficiency/performance of these robots.

Both of these applications are very exciting commercialization opportunities. As always, robotics business are usually more challenging and easier to fail. But these applications and challenges are necessary to conquer if we want to build commercialization today.
